**Open-source AI navigation glasses under US$35 that say what they see — and say "uncertain" instead of guessing.**
Repo: https://github.com/DresdenGman/AIGlasses_for_navigation · Build guide (CC BY): OER Commons · Archive: DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23977544

## What exists today

- A working, costed design: US reference build priced at US$32.29 against a public bill of materials (under US$20 in China); printable v0.4 enclosure; MIT-licensed code.
- A real user base with published data: 20 units in 3 communities; 85 feedback responses with raw data and recomputation scripts public — helpfulness 3.47/5, 9 of 64 users still using, top problems inaccurate prompts (48%) and battery (36%).
- Two published perception measurements (a public benchmark and a recorded near-field run) that document limits in the open, including a red-light misread. Limits are part of the record, not fine print.

## Three concrete ways to work together

1. **Replicate it.** The build is documented end to end, and an open issue (repo Issue #14) invites independent replication with a fixed protocol: build from the public docs only, log where they fail you, publish the log either way. Zero independent replications exist so far — the first one, successful or not, is the single most valuable contribution anyone can make right now.
2. **Host a build workshop.** Sessions are the distribution channel that has actually worked: participants assemble a unit and test it the same day. Materials run to roughly US$39 per participant at US prices (estimate from the costed bill of materials, including consumables). The bilingual guide and session format are public; we can share the facilitation notes.
3. **Collect near-field frames with us.** The published evaluation gap is glasses-height, walking-pace imagery. A partner site that can host consented, redacted frame collection (protocol in docs/RECORDED_EVAL.md) closes the evidence gap that published photographs cannot.

## What a partner gets

- Everything public by default: data, code, results — including negative ones, credited to contributors.
- A design whose failure modes are already mapped, so collaboration starts from evidence rather than a pitch.
- A direct line to the builder; decisions and changes are logged in the open repo.

## What we will not do

- No claims of certification, clinical effect, or street safety — the device is a research prototype and is labelled as one everywhere.
- No charging end users for the glasses; sustainability, when it comes, is built on kits, workshops and sponsorship, not on the people the device is for.
